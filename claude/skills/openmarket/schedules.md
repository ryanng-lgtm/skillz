# om schedules

Guardrails that hold whichever section you read:

- A schedule delivers an artifact on exactly one WHEN of five: a recurring wall clock (`cron`+`tz`), a one-shot instant (`at`), a change monitor (`monitorCron`+`monitorTz` with one probe), an alert's fires (`alertId`), or an event watch's live events (`watchId`) (§"The five whens"). A market CONDITION is still authored as an alert (`skill_read("alerts")`); the condition when binds a delivery to that alert's fires, it never re-implements the condition. A standing news subject is a feed (`skill_read("news")`).
- The when is immutable after create: trigger and source id are fixed, a cron/tz edit on a trigger-driven spec is refused (`immutable_trigger`), and a new when means remove + re-create. The delivery fields (channel, workspace, caption) stay editable everywhere.
- Monitors probe PUBLIC http(s) URLs only (no private hosts, no credentials in the URL); every listing and show exposes the probe by its ORIGIN only, never the full URL. Named probes (`probeName`, e.g. `metric`) validate their args at create against the probe's own schema.
- Trigger-driven specs are `spec_version: 2`: a daemon older than this build skips them unread after a rollback (`schedule_list` flags each such row), and source rows that land during a rollback interval or a pause are skipped on re-arm (resume snapshots a fresh floor), never replayed.
- `cron` is a 5-field expression (`m h dom mon dow`) and `tz` MUST be an IANA zone name, never a raw offset: map "UTC+8" to a city zone like Asia/Singapore (Etc/GMT-8 also works, but Etc zones invert the sign). "Every morning" with no time named is a question, not a default.
- Exactly six kinds exist: `workspace_screenshot`, `errand`, `verb`, `news_digest`, `strategy_digest`, `worklog_digest`. `schedule_create` mints the first three; each digest is a singleton its own funnel manages (§"The six kinds"). Never invent another kind or promise one, and route a digest's cadence change to its funnel, never to a generic verb.
- An errand or verb runs with NO ONE watching, so it runs only under a human's approval: creating, editing, resuming or adopting one raises a card that states exactly what will run, and nothing is sealed until the human says yes (§"Errands and verbs"). Never claim one is armed before that yes, and never pass an `authorization` field yourself (the runtime strips it).
- State the cadence back in the user's words before creating ("a chart of your BTC workspace to Telegram daily at 07:30 Singapore time"), and verify after the create with `schedule_show`: relay the next fire instant and the destination, then stop.
- A schedule counts from creation: the first delivery is the next cron occurrence after now, never an immediate fire. The same rule holds on `schedule_resume`: slots that lapsed while paused or stood down are gone, not replayed.
- Sharing a schedule's runs is consent the user gives once per room, and the room sees only the allowlisted projection: the display label, the schedule kind and the coarse trigger kind, never the target, channel, workspace, URL or probe arguments (§"Sharing a schedule's runs"). `schedule_share` raises an approval card and posts under the user's own account; `schedule_unshare` is the safe direction and dispatches at once. Earlier schedule runs are not published: every share is future-only, whatever the ask.
- A failing schedule is reported as failing, from the receipts: `lastError` on the spec sticks until the next successful slot, `schedule_events` holds the per-slot ledger, and a stood-down schedule names its reason and is only revived by `om schedule resume <id>` (§"Receipts and recovery"). `lastFiredAt` is the consumed-slot cursor (stamped at create and resume, before anything has fired), never a delivery: the delivery clocks are `lastSuccessAt` and `lastAttemptAt`, and `om schedule list` prints `last=` from them.
- A package-origin schedule (installed from a `routine-pack`) lands PAUSED, and each kind has its own arm ritual: a screenshot member arms through a carded `schedule_resume` naming its channel, workspace, cadence and probe origin (one yes arms one member); an errand member arms only at a terminal, in two steps (`om schedule adopt <id>` shows the full prompt and seals it paused, then `om schedule resume <id>` enables it), so hand the user those commands instead of raising a resume card. A local schedule resumes card-free as before. Provenance lives in the schedules state-store sidecar, never as a key on the spec file (§"Routine packs").

Routing:

- A standing SUBJECT ("keep me posted on X", headlines, tweets) → the news lane: `om news catalog`, `skill_read("news")`. Its daily brief cadence is `om news brief --schedule <HH:MM|off>`, not a generic schedule verb.
- A market CONDITION ("when BTC crosses 100k") → `skill_read("alerts")`. A condition checked on a cadence is still an alert; the alert engine owns the checking clock. "And send my chart when it fires" is then a condition schedule bound to that alert (`alertId`).
- A wall-clock or triggered ARTIFACT ("chart screenshot every morning", "this chart once at 4pm", "send the chart when this page changes", "chart on every fire of alert 3") → `schedule_create` with the matching when (§"The five whens", §"Create a screenshot schedule").
- Unattended WORK on a when ("every morning check funding and message me what stands out", "each weekday at 9 send me the BTC markets read") → `schedule_create` with `kind: "errand"` (a prompt plus the tools it may call) or `kind: "verb"` (one action with fixed args), then relay the card's yes (§"Errands and verbs").
- A CADENCE BRIEF about a subject ("every weekday at 8, tell me only if X said something new, with links") is ONE errand and nothing else: `toolsAllow: ["web_research"]` (the wide default deliberately excludes the spender, so name it; add any reads the brief also needs), done in one card. Anchor the prompt to the cadence window ("cover roughly the last 24 hours" / "since the previous weekday run"): each firing is a fresh turn with no memory of what earlier firings reported. `web_research` seals only onto a model whose lane enforces the search cap (xai, anthropic, or an OpenAI API-key model): when the home default is a subscription lane (openai-codex), pass `model` with a cappable one in the SAME create instead of discovering the refusal afterwards. Do NOT run `watch_compose` or create event watches for this shape: the composer is for "watch X for me" (continuous collection with its own pings) and costs several approvals the brief never needed. Only when the user asks for BOTH continuous watching AND a scheduled digest do watches enter, composed silent (`notify: false`) so the errand's journal reads are the only voice; never create watches noisy and edit them quiet after.
- Digest cadences → their funnels: `om news brief --schedule`, `om strategy digest schedule set`, and the work-ledger's own enable/disable verbs for the worklog digest. The generic surface still lists, shows, pauses, resumes and removes them (worklog excepted: its lifecycle verbs refuse and point at the ledger's, save `schedule_resume` clearing a stand-down).
- "Post schedule 3's runs into #macro", "stop sharing it there" → `schedule_share` / `schedule_unshare` on the id and the room name (§"Sharing a schedule's runs"); publishing the runs to followers is the registry share (`om share schedule:<id>`, event-watches.md §"Share a watch or alert: streams, follow, fork, stats"), a separate consent with the same projection.
- "What is scheduled", "when does it fire next", "why did nothing arrive", "how reliable is it" → `schedule_list`, then `schedule_show` / `schedule_events` / `schedule_stats` on the id (§"Receipts and recovery"). `schedule_list` shows `enabled` only; whether a disabled entry is paused or stood down comes from `schedule_show`.
- "publish this schedule" / "share my morning routine" → `package_publish` with the source `schedule:<id>` (dry run first; a cron or monitor screenshot or errand schedule, and `with_watches` carries local event watches beside an errand). "What did that pack install", "arm the routines I installed" → `package_show_routine`, then per kind: one carded `schedule_resume` per screenshot member; for an errand hand the user the terminal two-step, `om schedule adopt <id>` then `om schedule resume <id>` (§"Routine packs").

## The six kinds

The kinds a schedule can carry, who creates each, and where it delivers; read this before naming a kind or routing a digest cadence.

| Kind | What fires | Created by | Destination |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `workspace_screenshot` | a chart-workspace PNG | `schedule_create` (`om schedule screenshot create`) | a named alert channel, required |
| `errand` | a headless agent turn: the sealed prompt, the sealed tool set, one bounded reply | `schedule_create` (`om schedule errand create`), sealed by a human's yes | a named channel, required (email included once configured) |
| `verb` | one unattended dispatch of a sealed action with sealed args (a read, or a frozen `order_place` / `order_cancel`) | `schedule_create` (`om schedule verb create`), sealed by a human's yes | a named channel, required |
| `news_digest` | the cross-feed daily brief | `om news brief --schedule` | bound channel, the default at fire time, or local-only |
| `strategy_digest` | the strategy fleet briefing | `om strategy digest schedule set` | its funnel's binding; unbound means local by design |
| `worklog_digest` | the work-ledger digest | the ledger's enable verb (`worklog_enable`) | an OM Rooms ledger channel, in its payload |

## The five whens

The trigger shapes `schedule_create` takes, exactly one per schedule; read this before picking a when or explaining why one fired, expired, or stayed silent.

| When | Fields | How it fires |
| --- | --- | --- |
| cron | `cron` + `tz` | every occurrence, from creation forward; the v1 spec shape, the only when a daemon rolled back to an older build keeps running (every other when writes a spec it drops unread) |
| at | `at` (ISO 8601) | once, within a 120s grace of the instant; later than grace it retires unfired with the reason written; after it fires it retires |
| monitor | `monitorCron` + `monitorTz` + `probeUrl` or `probeName`+`probeArgs` | probes on the cadence; the first successful probe settles a silent baseline, each later content change fires once (delivery is at-least-once) |
| condition | `alertId` | on each fire of that local alert, from the binding's floor forward |
| event | `watchId` | on each LIVE event of that local event watch; backfill and catch-up rows never fire |

- Rules that hold across the trigger whens: the when and its source id are immutable (remove + re-create to change), a retired generation never re-arms (`schedule_resume` refuses with `schedule_retired` and names `schedule_create`), and pause/remove cancel queued firings rather than parking them.
- Monitor probes are public-only by design: create refuses private hosts, userinfo and non-http(s), and every read surface shows a URL probe as its origin alone. Named probes run registered adapters, and the registry holds one: `metric`.
- A condition/event schedule created before its alert or watch exists warns ("does not exist yet") and arms anyway; `schedule_show` reports the binding with its floor and backlog once the source exists.

## Create a screenshot schedule

The `schedule_create` call for a recurring chart-workspace PNG; read this when the user wants a chart delivered at a set time.

`schedule_create` takes `kind: "workspace_screenshot"`, `cron`, `tz`, `channelName`, and `payload: {workspaceId, caption?}`. The channel must already be configured (`om setup list`; pair one with `om setup <channel>`), the workspace id comes from `om chart list` or a share link, and screenshot fires are rate limited to 1 per minute per user. `enabled: false` (`--paused` on the CLI) creates it dormant for a later `schedule_resume`.

Stagger: on periods of an hour or longer a fire lands up to five minutes after the nominal cron instant (a deterministic per-home offset that keeps fleets off one synchronized wave); `schedule_show` reports the effective instant with the offset. Sub-hourly cadences fire on the nominal clock.

## Errands and verbs

Unattended work on a when: what an errand or verb is, what the card the human approves covers, how one stands down, and how a hand-authored file is adopted.

- An errand is a fresh headless turn per firing: no transcript, no memory, no chat context, the sealed `prompt` as untrusted instructions, the trigger's observation as a separate untrusted field, and ONLY the sealed `toolsAllow` set (strictly resolved: a name that does not resolve refuses the whole firing, nothing is silently dropped). It runs under `budgetMs` and `min(maxToolCalls, 32)` and delivers one bounded reply to `channelName` (or nothing, when it has nothing to say). A verb is one dispatch of `action` with `args` and delivers the result.
- Reads are wide by default: omitting `toolsAllow` seals every audited read-only tool (om market/metric data, Hyperliquid and Polymarket account reads, execution receipts, event-journal reads, symbol/market resolution) as a concrete list, and the card states it in one line. Do NOT enumerate reads or guess a minimal set; pass `toolsAllow` only when the user asked to restrict (any limiting phrasing), and `[]` for a reply-only errand. `web_research` is never part of the default: name it only when the task needs hosted search (it spends the configured AI credential each firing, capped per call by `agent.web_search.max_calls`; the card says so, and adds a warning when account reads ride the same run). Journal and receipt text arrives fenced as stored untrusted content, and unattended journal reads cover `events.md` only. Anything unaudited or reaching capital, generation, publication, scheduling, live third-party fetch or vendor spend is refused at create with the name (`tool_unresolved`); do not retry with a different spelling, tell the user what an errand may call.
- A VERB may additionally seal one of the two frozen capital verbs, `order_place` and `order_cancel`, with its exact args: the card states the whole call as it dispatches (defaults included), the venue and the network the home is paired to now, the per-fire bound, and that the one yes stands until the schedule is paused or removed. The shapes whose bound the card can state are a quote-mode size (a USD value) at market on either side or on a limit BUY, and a base size with a limit price; a base-size market order, a quote-sized limit SELL (its dollars follow the fill price, so size that one in base units), a `pct_equity` or `position` size (the account decides those at fire time), `caps`, and any order whose per-fire value sits under the venue's $10 minimum refuse the seal (`frozen_call_unstateable`; the refusal names which bar it hit, and for a wrong SHAPE the hint carries the shape to use instead, while a sub-minimum order needs a bigger per-fire size, not a different shape), and a cancel must name `venue: "hyperliquid"` (Polymarket cancels are not admitted), so shape the call that way rather than retrying. Each firing submits the order once, under a client order id derived from the firing, with no re-submit, no slicing and no replay below it, and nothing sent past the firing's deadline; a failure or an unknown outcome is delivered to the channel instead, and the next slot is a fresh order. An errand can never name these verbs.
- The consent story: `schedule_create` and `schedule_resume` on an errand or verb always raise a card, an edit touching any execution-affecting field raises one wherever it is made, and `om schedule adopt <id>` confirms the same terms at the terminal. The card states the prompt or the exact action call, the cadence, the sealed model, the reads line (one summary line for the wide default, the named list when restricted, marking any name unattended work may never call, which the seal refuses), the per-firing caps, and the destination channel with its generation. The yes covers exactly those terms: they are digested, and a firing whose file no longer matches (a hand-edited prompt, model, cadence, tool set, or a re-targeted channel) stands the schedule down with the reason on `schedule_show`. A declined, timed-out or cardless call seals nothing. Never pass `authorization` yourself.
- An edit through chat can only NARROW what the errand may call (the sealed reach becomes the old reach intersected with the newly approved set). Widening is a terminal act: `om schedule adopt <id>` re-seals the file's full scope after a confirm. A `label` edit needs no card.
- `schedule_show` on an errand or verb reports `authority` (state `active`, `paused`, `stood_down`, `revoked`, or `transitioning`, its revision, the sealed tool count, and the reason). `nextFireBasis: "unsealed"` means a hand-authored file no human has adopted: say so and point at `om schedule adopt <id>`. Pause deactivates the authority before the file is touched (a hand flip of `enabled` re-arms nothing); remove tombstones the generation for good (a restored file never fires and adopt refuses it); resume is the only reactivation and issues a new revision.
- Listings and show carry the label, the tool COUNT, the budgets, the model and the action name, never the prompt, the tool names or the args: relay a schedule by its label and cadence, not by quoting a prompt you cannot see.
- MCP cannot author an errand or verb (the create/edit/resume shapes are refused there and adopt is a terminal-only verb, never listed); a chat with a card or the `om schedule` CLI are the only doors.

## Receipts and recovery

The read verbs (`schedule_show`, `schedule_events`, `schedule_stats`) and what a stand-down means; read this when asked what fires next or why nothing arrived.

- `schedule_show` is the verification read: the sanitized spec view, the destination, the next fire with its basis, the monitor baseline / source binding / queue when the when has one, the last recorded run, and any stand-down or retirement fact. `nextFireBasis` is `scheduled` (nominal + stagger, an `at` instant, or a monitor's next probe, ahead of now), `due` (its instant passed and the daemon runs it on its next pass, so a `due` that persists means the daemon is down: `om service status`), `retry` (a live same-slot retry with its attempt clock), `source` (a condition/event schedule: it fires when its source does, no clock), or names why there is none: `expired` (an `at` past its grace, retiring unfired), `retired`, `paused`, `stood_down`, `cron_error`.
- `schedule_events` is the merged run ledger, newest first: cron slots (`ledger: "slot"`, one row per slot's latest attempt with `ok` / `error` / `stood_down`) and trigger runs (`ledger: "trigger"`, one row per at/monitor/condition/event firing with `ok` / `error` / `expired` / `overflowed` / `cancelled`) in one list. Use it before speculating about why a delivery did not arrive.
- `schedule_stats` aggregates the retained ledger: fires, errors, on-time rate, first fire, last success, stand-downs. When `dataComplete` is false the counts are floors (retention pruned history), never totals. Its `fireOutbox` counts the successful runs handed to the run mirror (`pending`, `mirrored`, `discarded` for runs before the first share, `quarantined` after five failed mirror attempts; there is no retry verb, quarantined rows stay visible until retention) and `shares` holds one receipts row per room share (delivered, suppressed, pending above the cursor, attempts and the last error).
- `schedule_remove` on a schedule whose runs are published live refuses with `stream_lane_archive_failed` when the relay cannot archive its lane: nothing is deleted, and the remove is retried once the relay answers. A successful remove revokes every room share of the generation with it.
- A slot is consumed when it fires or terminally fails; slots missed during an outage collapse into the latest one. Transient screenshot failures (network, timeout, 5xx, rate limit) retry the SAME slot on a backoff ladder until the next natural slot approaches; digest kinds record the failure and move on.
- Repeated failures stand a schedule down (10 straight failed runs for screenshots, 3 straight cron evaluation failures for any kind): it flips to `enabled: false` (the worklog digest excepted: its flag belongs to the ledger's verbs, so only `schedule_show` reveals its stand-down), keeps the reason in the sidecar (`schedule_show` reports it; `schedule_list` shows no more than the flag), and sends a notice carrying the literal `om schedule resume <id>`. Resume clears the streaks and arms the next occurrence after now; report a stand-down as "stood down after N failures: reason", never as "paused". A stood-down worklog digest is re-armed the same way: `schedule_resume` clears its stand-down and leaves its enabled flag as the ledger's verbs set it (a plainly disabled one takes `om worklog enable`, not resume).

## Sharing a schedule's runs

`schedule_share` binds one schedule to one OM Chat room so its runs post there as cards; read this before sharing, explaining a card, or answering why a run never showed in a room.

- One card per successful run committed after the share, never earlier runs (`SCHEDULE_HISTORY_LINE` on every consent surface: "Earlier schedule runs are not published."). The card carries three facts and nothing else: the display label, the schedule kind and the trigger kind (cron, at, monitor, condition, event), plus the run's age. Never the target, channel, workspace, URL, probe arguments, source ids, captions or excerpts.
- At most one card per 10 minutes per room; the rest are recorded as suppressed receipts, visible in `schedule_stats` under `shares`, never posted later. Cards post under the operator's account only while that account is signed in (otherwise they wait, nothing is lost).
- A room failure retries under the same message id (no duplicates); five consecutive failures park the share until `om schedule share <id> --room <room>` re-arms it. A re-armed share posts future runs only.
- Several schedules into one room = ONE call: `schedule_share` / `schedule_unshare` take `ids` with ONE `room` (`om schedule share <id> <id> --room <room>`); the card names the room once in its header and every schedule as a row, and the yes covers exactly that set.
- `schedule_unshare` (or `om news unshare`) revokes the room; `schedule_remove` revokes every room share of the generation; a re-created schedule id needs a fresh share (fresh id, fresh consent). `om news shares` lists schedule shares beside feed shares (provider `schedule`, no owed column: the cursor is the queue).
- The share requires a signed-in non-guest account and membership with posting rights in the room; a near-miss room name is refused with the rooms it nearly matched (`room_not_found`, `room_not_joined`, `room_not_postable`, `room_archived`), and a share owned by another account is refused (`share_owner_mismatch`).
## Routine packs

A schedule published as a package copy: `om publish schedule:<id>` ships one, an install lands members paused, and each member kind has its own arm ritual; read this before publishing or arming.

- **What ships.** A `routine-pack` member is a `workspace_screenshot` or `errand` schedule on a `cron` when or a `monitor` when whose probe is a public URL by its ORIGIN ROOT (no path, no query) or a built-in metric with a closed selector, plus optional carried event watches (`watches/<slug>.json`, the event-watch pack's own authoring form) so an errand arrives beside the sources it reads. `at`, condition and event whens, verb schedules (their sealed args are home-specific), every digest kind, WRUN metric ids and dependencies refuse at publish, at install and at the registry. Every surface prints a URL probe by origin only.
- **Needs, not resources.** A pack carries no channel and no workspace: publishing maps `channelName` to `needs.channel` and, for a screenshot, `payload.workspaceId` to `needs.workspace` (each a description the pack author writes, 160 chars); an errand declares `needs.channel` only, and a watch member declares none. The installing home answers the needs once for every member. The publish result's `notes` names the mapped and stripped fields, never their values.
- **What never ships.** An errand's sealed model (the installing home's default model runs it), its channel, and its authority (the installer seals its own arm at `om schedule adopt`); a carried watch's group label, `notify`, `enabled` and history. Say so when a user asks what a pack exposes.
- **Publish.** `package_publish` with the source `schedule:<id>` (CLI: `om publish schedule:<id>`; a bare id never resolves a schedule), `dry_run=true` first, the real publish on explicit intent through the same card as every kind. `with_watches` (CLI: repeatable `--with-watch <slug-or-id>`; the schedule ref form only) carries LOCAL event watches into the pack as `watches/<slug>.json`: a watch the user follows but does not own refuses `not_owned`, and duplicates collapse by watch id.
